16v16 Chess: how to play

It's chess, played by a team. Every piece belongs to a person, you can only move your own, and whoever moves first on your team's turn makes the move for everyone.

Ordinary chess rules apply to anything not covered here.

The short version
  1. When it's your team's turn, anyone on the team can move one of their own pieces.
  2. The first legal move to reach the server is the team's move. Everyone else is too late.
  3. There are no take-backs, and every move has the mover's name on it.

Your pieces

Each piece has a coloured disc and a name tag showing who owns it. Yours have a brighter tag with a white outline. You can't touch anyone else's pieces, including your teammates'.

How many pieces you get depends on the room:

GamePlayers per sideYou control
2 v 22Half the board: the queenside (a to d files) or the kingside (e to h files)
4 v 44Two neighbouring pieces plus the two pawns in front of them
16 v 1616One piece

If a side has fewer players than seats, the pieces from empty seats are shared out among the players who are there. Two people can play any size of game.

Your pieces stay yours all game. A pawn you promote is still yours.

Making a move

  • Drag a piece to a square, or tap the piece and then tap the square.
  • Dots show where the piece can go. A ring means a capture.
  • To change your mind before letting go, press Esc or drop the piece back where it started. Once you let go on a legal square, the move is sent.
  • Your pieces turn faded when they have no legal moves.

As soon as a move lands, the mover's name flashes across the board for everyone, and it goes into the move list, for example "Ray: Bc4". If a teammate beat you to it, your move is thrown away. Those near-misses are counted, and you'll see them at the end.

Check

When your king is in check, only pieces that can get it out of check can move. They're outlined in orange, and the status bar names their owners. Everyone else is locked out and can only watch.

Sometimes the whole team is relying on one person. The clock keeps running.

Checkmate loses the game. If your side has no legal move and isn't in check, it's stalemate, which is a draw.

Special moves

  • Castling is a king move, so the king's owner decides. The rook goes along and its owner gets credited as an accomplice in the move list.
  • En passant belongs to the pawn making the capture.
  • Promotion: when your pawn reaches the far end, you get 5 seconds to pick a queen, rook, bishop or knight. The team clock keeps running. If you don't pick, you get a queen.

The clock

Each side's time runs while it's their turn. The default is 3 minutes per side, plus 2 seconds added after each move. The host can change both in the lobby.

If your clock runs out you lose, unless the other side doesn't have enough pieces left to checkmate, in which case it's a draw.

The king's player

Whoever owns the king (👑 in the player list) holds the Resign and Offer draw buttons for the whole team, and answers the other side's draw offers. Everyone else gets Call for resign and Call for draw, which ask the king's player in team chat.

Losing all your pieces

If your last piece is captured you become a ghost 👻. You stay on the team and can still chat, but you can't move. There's no coming back.

Chat

In the lobby everyone shares one chat. Once the game starts, each team has its own chat that the other side can't see. Spectators can watch but can't post during a game.

Talking out loud or on a voice call is fine. Shouting at whoever just moved is part of the game.

Bots

The host can fill empty seats with bots 🤖. They play at roughly beginner-club level, so expect them to hang pieces now and then. A bot only moves its own pieces, and it waits a few seconds before moving, so you can beat it to the move. To replace a bot with a person, that person clicks the bot's seat in the lobby.

If a bot holds your king, any human on your side can use the Resign and Offer draw buttons.

Setting up a game

  1. Enter your name and press Create room. Pick the size first.
  2. Share the 4-letter room code, or press Copy invite link.
  3. Everyone clicks a seat on White or Black. Unseated players watch. The host can press Shuffle to deal everyone into random seats, and Fill empty seats with bots to make up the numbers.
  4. The host sets the clock and presses Start game. Each side needs at least one player.

After a game the host can take everyone back to the lobby for another one.

If you get disconnected

The page reconnects by itself, and refreshing puts you back in your seat. Don't close the tab during a game. A new tab counts as a new player, who can't get back to the old pieces. While you're gone, your pieces stay put and nobody else can move them. After 15 seconds you show as 💤 away.
